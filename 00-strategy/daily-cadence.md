**Daily Plan: Three Segments, Run in Parallel**

*A day-by-day breakdown for running all three segments side by side, six-plus focused hours a day, instead of sequential two-week sprints.*

# How This Plan Is Structured

Running three segments in parallel changes the unit of work from "two weeks on one segment" to "every day touches all three." With 6+ focused hours available, each day splits into three roughly 90-minute to 2-hour blocks — one per segment — plus a short recurring admin/sync block. This avoids the common failure mode of parallel work: everything gets a little attention and nothing gets finished, because each block below ends in a concrete, closeable task rather than open-ended "work on X."

- Detailed days: anything involving content creation (writing, filming, designing) gets full task-level detail, since that's where time actually disappears unpredictably.

- Light days: admin, outreach follow-up, and review days get a short list — these don't need hour-by-hour breakdown to be useful.

- Color key used throughout: Blue = Existing Customers, Orange = DTG/DTF New, Green = Distributors, Grey = Admin/Sync.

# A Few Notes on Making This Realistic

- If any single block consistently overruns its time, that's signal, not failure — move that segment's heavier days to Tuesday/Thursday and lighten Monday for it the following week.

- The Wednesday sync is the one block that should never get cut, even on a slow week — it's what keeps Sales feedback flowing into the messaging in real time, rather than discovered three weeks later.

- If a week genuinely can't fit all three segments (illness, urgent fire, trade show), drop Distributors first and DTG/DTF second — existing customer communication has the shortest "window of relevance" once neoStampa 2026 is no longer new.

# 4. Social Media — Standalone Track

Social runs across all three segments rather than belonging to one, so it sits outside the daily segment blocks. Treat it as a 30–45 minute recurring task layered onto the existing week, not a fourth full block — the content mostly repurposes what the segment work is already producing.

## Weekly Rhythm

- Monday (15 min): scan what's shipping this week across all three segments (email, video, one-pager) and pick 2–3 moments worth posting.

- Wednesday (20 min, paired with the sync block): turn one piece of in-progress work into a short post — a behind-the-scenes clip of a demo video being filmed, a sneak peek of the distributor one-pager design, a quote pulled from the customer ROI story.

- Friday (15 min, paired with ship/review): post the finished asset from that week — the published demo video, the sent customer email reframed as a public post, or a thank-you/recap if an exhibition just happened.

## What Belongs Here vs. Elsewhere

- Repurpose, don't originate: social content should almost always be a smaller cut of something the segment tracks already made, not a separate creative effort competing for time.

- Platform priority for this audience: LinkedIn first (where distributors, decision-makers, and existing B2B customers actually are), YouTube for demo videos themselves, Instagram only for visual/behind-the-scenes content if time allows — don't split effort evenly across platforms by default.

- Track one metric only for now: engagement on posts that link to a trial or demo request, not follower count or likes — vanity metrics here would recreate the exact problem this whole plan is trying to fix.

# 5. Demo Video & Tutorial Creation — Standalone Track

This is distinct from the DTG/DTF segment's acquisition-focused demo videos (Section 3, weeks 1–9) — tutorials serve existing customers and trial users who need to actually succeed with the software, which directly affects both retention and trial-to-paid conversion.

## Recurring Cadence

- One tutorial every 2 weeks, alternating between a core workflow topic (color profiling, calibration, cost control) and a frequently-asked support question pulled directly from Sales or support tickets.

- Production pattern mirrors the content-day structure already in use: script Monday, film/screen-record Tuesday, edit Wednesday/Thursday, publish Friday — but as a lighter parallel task (60–90 min/day) layered onto whichever segment day has the most room that week.

## Source List to Build From First

- Ask Sales for the 5 questions they get asked most often during onboarding calls — these become the first 5 tutorial topics, guaranteeing relevance without guessing.

- Mine existing support tickets or FAQ pages, if they exist, for recurring friction points worth turning into a 2–3 minute walkthrough.

- Reuse footage: if a DTG/DTF acquisition demo (Section 3) already shows a workflow step clearly, repurpose that clip into a tutorial rather than reshooting from scratch.

## Distribution

- Publish to YouTube as the permanent home, then link tutorials directly from the customer-facing knowledge base or onboarding emails, not just socially — these are reference content people come back to, not one-time announcements.

# 6. Exhibition & Trade Show Planning — Standalone Track

With 2 or more exhibitions expected in this 90-day window, each one needs its own mini-timeline rather than being squeezed into a single week — exhibition prep realistically starts 4–6 weeks out and has a distinct "during" and "after" phase that's easy to underestimate.

## Per-Exhibition Timeline (repeat for each show)

| **Wk** | **Existing Customers** | **DTG/DTF New** | **Distributors** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T-6** | Confirm booth logistics with Sales/ops; decide which segment(s) this show targets (often Distributors + DTG/DTF, less often Existing Customers) | Block calendar time now for T-2 and T-1 weeks — these always take longer than expected | Notify distributor contacts being targeted that you'll be at the show |
| **T-4** | Finalize what's being demoed live (likely the same DTG/DTF workflow from the acquisition video, performed in person) | Order/check any printed material — reuse the distributor one-pager design rather than creating new collateral | Draft the "come see us at [show]" social and email announcement |
| **T-2** | Rehearse the live demo; confirm any hardware/printer needs with the venue or partner | Finalize the pre-show outreach list: who specifically should know you'll be there | Pack/ship materials with buffer time for delays |
| **T-1** | Send the "come see us" announcement across email and social | Confirm booth setup logistics one last time | Brief whoever is attending (if not solo) on key talking points per segment |

## During the Show

- Capture short video/photo content live — this becomes both a same-day social post and raw material for a recap piece afterward, so don't treat documentation as an afterthought.

- Log leads and conversations in a simple running list (company, contact, segment, what they asked about) — without this, the most valuable output of the show (warm leads) gets lost in memory within a week.

## After the Show (within 1 week)

- Follow up individually with every lead captured, referencing the specific conversation — generic "thanks for visiting" emails convert far worse than a remembered detail.

- Publish a short recap post/email — this is good, low-effort social content (Section 4) that also signals momentum to people who weren't there.

- Fold any new objections or questions heard at the show into the next relevant segment's messaging — exhibitions are one of the few moments you get unfiltered, in-person reactions to how Inèdit's pitch actually lands.

# 7. New Release Launch Playbook — General

Since a specific release date isn't confirmed for this window, this section is a reusable playbook rather than a dated plan — if and when a release is confirmed, slot its timeline directly into the 12-week cadence (Section 3) using the structure below, prioritizing whichever segment(s) the release most affects.

## Pre-Launch (2–3 weeks before)

- Get the real feature list and what problem each feature solves, in plain language, directly from product/engineering — translate technical changes into the segment-specific benefit language already established in this plan (speed for DTG/DTF, reliability for distributors, ROI for existing customers).

- Identify which segment benefits most from this specific release — not every release matters equally to all three, and the launch effort should weight accordingly rather than treating every release as equally relevant to everyone.

- Reuse the existing neoStampa announcement email template (already built) as the base structure, swapping in the new release's specific features rather than designing from scratch each time.

## Launch Week

- Email to existing customers first (they're warmest and most likely to upgrade quickly), then a public announcement (social, website) within the same week.

- If the release timing lines up with Section 5's tutorial cadence, prioritize a tutorial on the single most-requested new feature over the regular alternating topic that week.

- If the release timing lines up with an upcoming exhibition (Section 6), the live demo should showcase the new release specifically — this is one of the highest-leverage moments to combine two workstreams into one effort.

## Post-Launch (1–2 weeks after)

- Track adoption of the new feature specifically, not just general trial/upgrade numbers — a release that doesn't get used wasn't actually marketed well, even if the announcement email had strong open rates.

- Fold any support questions or confusion about the new feature directly into the next tutorial topic (Section 5) — new releases are a guaranteed source of fresh, relevant tutorial material.
