**90-Day Marketing Plan**

*A focused, accountable plan to replace scattered activity with a clear strategy, measurable outcomes, and room to be creative again.*

Prepared for: Marketing & Communications Director, Inèdit Software, S.L.

Scope: Existing customers · New DTG/DTF & garment decorators · Printer-brand & distributor partnerships

Resourcing: Marketing lead, in close collaboration with Sales

# 0. Why This Plan Exists

This plan starts from a specific diagnosis, not a generic template: the issue isn't a shortage of ideas or effort, it's the absence of a strategy that tells you which work matters and a scoreboard that proves it. Email newsletters like the recent neoStampa 2026 feature announcement are well-built, but a single channel run on repeat, without segmentation or a measurement layer behind it, will always feel like effort without traction.

Three things this plan is built to fix:

- No segmentation: "the textile industry" is a category, not a strategy. Three different buyers need three different messages and different proof points.

- No scoreboard: opens and impressions don't tell you if the business is moving. Trials, qualified demos, and partner engagement do.

- No creative room: when every output is reactive, there's no space left to do the work that actually feels good to make.

Everything below is designed to be run by one person working closely with Sales — nothing here assumes a team you don't have.

# 1. The Three Segments (and What Each One Actually Needs)

## 1.1 Existing Customers — Retain & Upsell

These customers already trust Inèdit's color management. The opportunity is moving them onto neoStampa 2026's new capabilities (5× faster PDF engine, remote queue management, fluorescent ink support) before a competitor's renewal pitch reaches them first.

- Primary message: "Your investment in neoStampa keeps paying off — here's what's new in your toolkit."

- Proof point needed: a quantified before/after (time saved, reprints avoided, color-match accuracy).

- Risk if ignored: quiet churn at renewal, since satisfied-but-unengaged customers are the easiest for competitors to poach.

## 1.2 New DTG/DTF & Garment Decorators — Acquisition

This is the fastest-growing, least loyal segment — many are recent entrants to digital textile printing who haven't settled on a RIP yet. They're price- and ease-of-use sensitive, often discovering tools via YouTube and peer forums rather than trade press.

- Primary message: "Stop fighting white-ink and color drift — neoStampa's DTG/DTF mode was built for exactly this."

- Proof point needed: short demo videos showing real garments, not abstract color charts.

- Risk if ignored: this segment defaults to whatever RIP came bundled with their printer.

## 1.3 Printer-Brand & Distributor Partners — Channel Growth

This segment doesn't buy neoStampa directly — they recommend or bundle it. The relationship is built on technical reliability and co-marketing, not promotional messaging.

- Primary message: "NeoStampa as the color-management layer that makes your hardware look its best, out of the box."

- Proof point needed: case studies and certifications showing compatibility with their specific printer lines.

- Risk if ignored: distributors default to a competitor's bundled RIP, and Inèdit never gets in front of the end customer at all.

# 2. The Scoreboard: What "Working" Actually Looks Like

Pick a small number of metrics and report them the same way every two weeks. The goal isn't more data — it's a consistent way to tell, at a glance, whether the last sprint of work moved the business.

| **Metric** | **Baseline** | **90-Day Target** | **Owner** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Trial signups (neoStampa)** | Set in week 1 | **+30%** | Marketing |
| **Qualified demo requests** | Set in week 1 | **+25%** | Marketing + Sales |
| **Existing-customer upgrade rate** | Set in week 1 | **20% of base engaged** | Marketing |
| **Active distributor co-marketing deals** | Set in week 1 | **2 new agreements** | Marketing |
| **Newsletter → trial click-through** | Set in week 1 | **+15%** | Marketing |

*Note: **"**Set in week 1**"** is intentional — Phase 1 below includes establishing real baselines before promising specific lifts. Treat the targets above as directional until baseline data confirms them.*

# 3. The 90-Day Arc

## Phase 1 (Weeks 1–2): Baseline & Focus

Before any new campaign work: pull current numbers for each KPI above, audit the last 6 months of content/email to see what actually drove trials or demos (not just opens), and get 30 minutes with Sales to hear, in their words, what objections come up most by segment.

## Phase 2 (Weeks 3–8): Build & Ship, Segment by Segment

Each segment gets a dedicated 2-week sprint with one clear deliverable, rather than three segments diluted across every week.

| **Week** | **Segment Focus** | **Action** | **Channel** | **Owner** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wk 3-4** | Existing customers | neoStampa 2026 upgrade campaign: case-study email + 1 short ROI video | Email + Website | Marketing |
| **Wk 5-6** | DTG/DTF decorators | 3-part "real garment" demo video series + targeted outreach list from Sales | YouTube + Email | Marketing + Sales |
| **Wk 7-8** | Distributor partners | Co-branded one-pager + outreach to 2 priority printer-brand contacts | Direct + PDF | Marketing + Sales |

## Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12): Measure, Double Down, Make Space

Review the scoreboard against Phase 1 baselines. Kill or fix whatever isn't moving the needle — don't let a quiet channel linger out of habit. Whatever segment shows the strongest response gets the bulk of weeks 11-12.

Reserve the final two weeks partly for one genuinely creative, lower-pressure project — not tied directly to a KPI target — as a deliberate counterweight to three months of disciplined, measured work. This is structural, not a reward: sustained energy needs a release valve built into the plan, not bolted on after burnout.

# 4. Operating Rhythm (So the Plan Survives Contact With Reality)

- Weekly: 15-minute sync with Sales — what objections or wins came up, any hot leads to fold into messaging.

- Bi-weekly: scoreboard review against this document — 20 minutes, same five metrics, no new ones added mid-quarter.

- End of each phase: a short written note (half a page) on what worked, what didn't, and what changes for next phase.

The point of this rhythm is to make the plan a living reference you actually open, not a document written once and ignored — which is usually how strategies quietly die.

# 5. What This Plan Deliberately Leaves Out

To stay realistic for one person working with Sales, this plan does not include: paid social campaigns, a full website overhaul, or a fourth segment. Any of these can be added in the next 90-day cycle once the scoreboard shows where energy is actually paying off — adding them now would dilute focus exactly where this plan is trying to create it.
